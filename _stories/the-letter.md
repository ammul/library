---
title: "The Letter"
date: 2026-05-25
---

The letter arrived on a Thursday, addressed in handwriting she did not recognize, with no return address and a postmark from a city she had never visited. She almost threw it away. She had a rule about letters with no return address.

She opened it instead.

*You don't know me,* it began, *but I knew your mother.*

Elena sat down at the kitchen table. Her mother had been dead for eleven years. She had died quietly, in the way of certain people who have spent a lifetime being quiet — a Tuesday afternoon, a hospital room, a hand that went still.

---

The letter was four pages, written front and back in a small, neat hand. It described a woman Elena did not recognize: a woman who laughed at bad jokes, who kept a notebook of pressed flowers, who once drove through the night to bring soup to a stranger's sick child.

*She never told you about me,* the letter said near the end. *She said it would only complicate things. But I thought you should know that she was loved. Not just by you. By a lot of people she never talked about.*

The letter was signed with initials only: *R.V.*

---

Elena read it three times. Then she folded it carefully and put it in the drawer where she kept her own notebook of things she didn't know what to do with.

She thought about her mother's hands. The way she had folded dish towels. The particular silence she kept while listening.

It occurred to Elena, sitting there in the ordinary light of a Thursday afternoon, that she had not known her mother at all. And that this was not a sad thing, exactly. It was simply a large thing, larger than she had room for right now, something she would have to return to later, the way you return to a painting that confused you the first time.

She closed the drawer. She put the kettle on. Outside, the day continued in its usual way, indifferent and bright.
