---
title: "The Fog"
date: 2026-05-20
---

It came in from the coast before dawn, the way it always did — silently, without warning, filling the hollows between the pines until the road disappeared and the town became a handful of orange lights floating in nothing.

Marta stood at the kitchen window with her coffee going cold. She had lived here forty years and never learned to trust the fog. Her husband said she was foolish. He said the fog was just water, just air, just weather. He said a lot of things before he walked into it one November and didn't come back.

---

The search party found his boots at the edge of the marsh. The laces were tied, which the deputy found strange, and which Marta found less strange than she wanted to admit.

She kept the boots in the hall closet now. Sometimes she opened the door just to look at them.

---

This morning the fog was thicker than usual. She could not see the mailbox. She could not see the road. She could hear the water dripping from the eaves, one drop at a time, a small and patient sound.

Then she heard something else.

Footsteps on the porch. Slow, considered, deliberate. The creak of the second board — the one she always meant to fix.

Marta set down her coffee. She did not call out. She did not move toward the door.

She had lived here forty years. She knew better than to open the door when the fog came in.
